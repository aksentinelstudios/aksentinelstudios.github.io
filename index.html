01 The studio

We start with the problem, not the product.

Most organisations are handed the same software and asked to adapt their processes to it. We take the opposite route: understand how the work actually happens, then design the smallest system that does it safely.

Our work sits where security engineering, applied intelligence and custom software meet — systems that are accountable for the data they hold, legible to the people who operate them, and honest about what they can and cannot do.

06 Research & development

Open questions we work on.

Research threads are published as they mature. Everything here is exploratory unless marked otherwise.

R-01Detection with less noise+

Signature and threshold detection fail in both directions: they miss slow, low-volume activity and they bury analysts in false positives. We are exploring behavioural baselines that model normal per-entity behaviour rather than global rules.

Focus · behavioural analytics, entity baselining

R-02Models that can explain themselves+

An automated decision that cannot be explained is difficult to defend and harder to audit. We evaluate where interpretable models are sufficient, and where a complex model needs a separate, verifiable explanation layer.

Focus · interpretability, decision provenance

R-03Least privilege as a default+

Permission sprawl is usually an interface problem, not a discipline problem. We prototype tooling that derives candidate permissions from observed behaviour and requires an explicit justification for every widening.

Focus · policy generation, access review

R-04Software that fits the workflow+

Custom software is only worth building when the operational detail matters. We document the mapping between a real process and the system that replaces it, so the reasoning survives after the original team moves on.

Focus · process modelling, system documentation

07 Engineering philosophy

How we build.

01

Smallest sufficient system

Every component is a liability as well as an asset. We add surface area only when the problem genuinely requires it, and remove it when it does not.

02

Explicit trust boundaries

Where data crosses from one domain to another, that crossing is named, authenticated and logged. Implicit trust is treated as a defect.

03

Operable by humans

A system nobody can inspect at 2am is not secure. Interfaces, logs and failure modes are designed for the people who will inherit them.
